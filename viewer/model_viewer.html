01 · Read the indices

In pi[n,i,j], what does each index identify?

Shares have shape (N,N,J). For each importer/industry, shares sum to one over exporters, axis 1.

02 · Match code to an equation

Select the block that computes the denominator of the trade-share equation in log space.

row_logsumexp sums over exporters within each importer/industry. It returns (N,J) log denominators; broadcasting [:,None,:] normalizes the (N,N,J) weights.

03 · Close the economy

Explain country-level market clearing, the damped wage update, and how normalized Cobb–Douglas utility defines cost of living.

Country sales sum pi[n,i,j]*alpha[n,j]*income[n] over importers and industries. They equal w[i]L[i]; individual industry trade need not balance. Damping multiplies wages by (sales/income)^0.2 before country-0 normalization. Utility product(c/alpha)^alpha has C=product P^alpha and real wage w/C; expenditure shares remain alpha.

04 · Change the numeraire

If all wages are rescaled by a common positive factor, what happens?

Industry prices and aggregate cost of living are homogeneous of degree one in wages. Common nominal scaling cancels from trade shares and w/C. Both verification routes must use the same wage normalization.

05 · Supply exact hats

Which information enters the exact-hat equilibrium equations for this trade-cost shock?

Hats reweight baseline shares by wage and trade-cost ratios using industry elasticities. Fixed preferences allocate country spending and aggregate industry price hats. Gamma cancels; the shocked costs are validated using retained primitives.

07 · Diagnose a false pass

All comparison errors are 1e−13 and wage changes are tiny, but the hat route reaches max_iterations with a full residual of 2e−4. What is the verdict?

Fail: small steps can reflect strong damping or stagnation. The iteration must reach its full residual target (default 1e−13), and verification requires all routes to converge, residual norms below 1e−11, and both error measures below 1e−9. An iteration-limit status is not convergence.

08 · Interpret the shock

Industry 0's bilateral costs fall 10%, while industry 1's costs stay fixed. Trace the effect on wages, both industries, aggregate prices and country 2's welfare.

Direct competitiveness changes in industry 0 alter demand and country incomes. Shared wages feed into both industries, so industry 1's shares and prices can respond without its own cost shock. C=product P^alpha weights those prices; welfare is w/C. Country 2 responds through world demand and wage adjustments.
