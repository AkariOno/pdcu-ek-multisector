Eaton–Kortum / one-industry baseline

One industry.
Two routes.
One equilibrium.

Follow the economics into the code. Solve a trade-cost counterfactual twice, inspect the evidence, then explain why the results agree.

3 countries1 factor · laborBalanced tradeWorks offline
The experiment

A 10% bilateral trade-cost cut between countries 0 and 1. Technology and labor stay fixed.

Compute changes through two full equilibria and independently through exact-hat algebra.

Acceptance: all three solves converge; every absolute and relative comparison error is below 10⁻⁹.

01 / Plan

What is being solved?

Relative wages clear labor-income markets. Trade shares and prices follow from delivered costs.

n · importer / rowi · exporter / column
Trade shares

pi[n,i] = T[i] (w[i] d[n,i])^(−theta)
/ sum_k T[k] (w[k] d[n,k])^(−theta)

Price index

P[n] = gamma [sum_i T[i] (w[i] d[n,i])^(−theta)]^(−1/theta)

Market clearing

w[i] L[i] = sum_n pi[n,i] w[n] L[n]

Primitives → full solution
Technology T[i], labor L[i], iceberg costs d[n,i], elasticity theta, and fixed price multiplier gamma = 1.
Baseline statistics + shock → exact hats
Expenditure shares pi0[n,i], incomes w0[i]L[i], theta, and trade-cost ratios d_hat[n,i].
Unknowns & numeraire
N−1 log wages (or log wage changes). Country 0 has w[0] = 1 in both levels equilibria and w_hat[0] = 1 in the hat route.
Imposed by construction
Importer share rows sum to one; expenditure equals labor income; the numeraire is exact.
Checked after solving
All-country normalized market-clearing residual norm < 10⁻¹¹, finite outputs, and successful optimizer termination.

One industry and one factor. No intermediates, tariffs, transfers, deficits, estimation, or performance optimization.

Levels route
Primitives → E0 & E1 → E1 / E0

Two solves, each starting from unit wages. The second uses shocked trade costs.

Exact-hat route
E0 + trade-cost hats → equilibrium hats

An independent solve from unit wage hats, using baseline shares and incomes.

Same shock + same closure + same numeraire → compare wage, price, share, and real-wage ratios

02 · Delivered unit costs

One wage per exporter scales its production cost, then bilateral iceberg costs determine the delivered cost.

c[n,i] = w[i] d[n,i]

src/ek_model/full_solution.py:13–22 · verbatim production excerpt · relevant lines highlighted

13def levels_objects(primitives: Primitives, log_free_wages):14    """Delivered unit costs determine importer-row shares and price indices."""15    wages = normalized_wages(log_free_wages)16    log_costs = np.log(wages)[None, :] + np.log(primitives.d)17    log_weights = np.log(primitives.T)[None, :] - primitives.theta * log_costs18    log_denominator = logsumexp(log_weights, axis=1)19    shares = np.exp(log_weights - log_denominator[:, None])20    prices = primitives.gamma * np.exp(-log_denominator / primitives.theta)21    incomes = wages * primitives.L22    return wages, prices, shares, incomes

04 · Price indices

The same denominator determines prices. gamma is a fixed positive multiplier and cancels from price changes.

P[n] = gamma (sum_i T[i] c[n,i]^(-theta))^(-1/theta)

05 · Market clearing

Transpose the share matrix to sum importer spending into each exporter's sales. This returns all N normalized residuals.

r[i] = (sum_n pi[n,i] income[n] - income[i]) / income[i]

src/ek_model/model.py:93–96 · verbatim production excerpt · relevant lines highlighted

93def market_clearing(shares: Array, incomes: Array) -> Array:94    """Sum importer spending down rows to obtain each exporter's sales."""95    sales = shares.T @ incomes96    return (sales - incomes) / incomes
06 · Equilibrium solver

Optimize N−1 log wages from zero, enforcing the numeraire exactly. Successful termination is necessary but the full residual and finite outputs also determine convergence.

solve r[1:] = 0; w[0] = 1; check max_i |r[i]| < 1e-11

01 · Baseline statistics & shock

Require a valid, converged baseline and positive trade-cost ratios. The resulting level costs must still be admissible iceberg costs.

pi0[n,i], income0[i], theta, d_hat[n,i]

src/ek_model/exact_hat.py:17–35 · verbatim production excerpt · relevant lines highlighted

17def validate_hat_inputs(baseline: Equilibrium, d_hat):18    """Require a converged baseline and an admissible trade-cost-only shock."""19    if not baseline.diagnostics.converged:20        raise ValueError("exact hats require a converged baseline")21    n = baseline.primitives.T.size22    shares = positive_array(baseline.shares, "baseline shares")23    incomes = positive_array(baseline.incomes, "baseline incomes")24    if shares.shape != (n, n) or incomes.shape != (n,):25        raise ValueError("baseline shares/incomes have invalid dimensions")26    if not np.allclose(shares.sum(axis=1), 1, rtol=0, atol=1e-12):27        raise ValueError("baseline share rows must sum to one")28    if np.max(np.abs(market_clearing(shares, incomes))) >= RESIDUAL_TOL:29        raise ValueError("baseline shares and incomes must clear markets")30    shock = positive_array(d_hat, "d_hat")31    if shock.shape != (n, n):32        raise ValueError("d_hat must have shape (N, N)")33    p = baseline.primitives34    Primitives(p.T, p.L, p.d * shock, p.theta, p.gamma)35    return shock
02 · Counterfactual shares

Reweight baseline shares using wage and trade-cost changes. This route does not recompute levels competitiveness from technology.

a[n,i] = pi0[n,i] (w_hat[i] d_hat[n,i])^(-theta); pi1[n,i] = a[n,i]/sum_k a[n,k]

src/ek_model/exact_hat.py:38–48 · verbatim production excerpt · relevant lines highlighted

38def hat_objects(baseline: Equilibrium, d_hat, log_free_hats):39    """The log row sum is log(sum_i pi0[n,i]*(w_hat[i]*d_hat[n,i])^-theta)."""40    wage_hats = normalized_wages(log_free_hats)41    log_a = np.log(baseline.shares) - baseline.primitives.theta * (42        np.log(wage_hats)[None, :] + np.log(d_hat)43    )44    log_row_sum = logsumexp(log_a, axis=1)45    shares = np.exp(log_a - log_row_sum[:, None])46    price_hats = np.exp(-log_row_sum / baseline.primitives.theta)47    incomes = baseline.incomes * wage_hats48    return wage_hats, price_hats, shares, incomes

04 · Hat-market residual

Labor is fixed, so wage hats also scale incomes. Demand and exporter sales must balance at the new shares.

income1[i] = income0[i] w_hat[i]; sales1[i] = income1[i]

51def hat_residual(log_free_hats, baseline: Equilibrium, d_hat):52    """New sales must equal baseline income multiplied by each wage hat."""53    _, _, shares, incomes = hat_objects(baseline, d_hat, log_free_hats)54    return market_clearing(shares, incomes)[1:]

57def solve_exact_hat(baseline: Equilibrium, d_hat) -> HatEquilibrium:58    """Solve log wage changes independently, fixing country 0's wage hat to 1."""59    shock = validate_hat_inputs(baseline, d_hat)60    result = least_squares(61        hat_residual, np.zeros(baseline.incomes.size - 1), args=(baseline, shock),62        ftol=SOLVER_TOL, xtol=SOLVER_TOL, gtol=SOLVER_TOL, max_nfev=MAX_NFEV,63    )64    with np.errstate(over="ignore", divide="ignore", invalid="ignore"):65        wage_hats, price_hats, shares, incomes = hat_objects(baseline, shock, result.x)66        real_wage_hats = wage_hats / price_hats67        residual = market_clearing(shares, incomes)68    diagnostics = make_diagnostics(69        result, residual, wage_hats, price_hats, shares, incomes, real_wage_hats,70    )71    return HatEquilibrium(72        wage_hats, price_hats, shares, incomes, real_wage_hats, residual, diagnostics,73    )
06 · Full convergence check

99def make_diagnostics(result, residual: Array, *outputs: Array) -> Diagnostics:100    """Optimizer success alone never certifies economic convergence."""101    finite = all(np.all(np.isfinite(x)) for x in (residual, *outputs))102    norm = float(np.max(np.abs(residual))) if finite else float("inf")103    return Diagnostics(104        converged=bool(result.success and finite and norm < RESIDUAL_TOL),105        solver_success=bool(result.success),106        status=int(result.status),107        message=str(result.message),108        nfev=int(result.nfev),109        residual_norm=norm,110    )

05 · Apply both strict bounds

The denominator is the levels-route ratio, not the change minus one. The two error tests are separate, so one cannot compensate for the other.

max |candidate-reference| < 1e-9 AND max |candidate-reference|/|reference| < 1e-9

27def compare_changes(reference, candidate):28    """Both strict bounds must pass; relative errors use the levels-route ratio."""29    reference = np.asarray(reference, dtype=float)30    candidate = np.asarray(candidate, dtype=float)31    valid = (32        reference.shape == candidate.shape and reference.size > 033        and np.all(np.isfinite(reference)) and np.all(np.isfinite(candidate))34        and np.all(reference != 0)35    )36    if not valid:37        return {"max_absolute_error": None, "max_relative_error": None,38                "tolerance": ERROR_TOL, "passed": False}39    with np.errstate(over="ignore", invalid="ignore", divide="ignore"):40        difference = np.abs(candidate - reference)41        absolute = float(difference.max())42        relative = float((difference / np.abs(reference)).max())43    finite = np.isfinite(absolute) and np.isfinite(relative)44    return {45        "max_absolute_error": absolute if np.isfinite(absolute) else None,46        "max_relative_error": relative if np.isfinite(relative) else None,47        "tolerance": ERROR_TOL,48        "passed": bool(finite and absolute < ERROR_TOL and relative < ERROR_TOL),49    }

01 · Read the indices

In pi[n, i], what does each index identify?

Each row is one importer's allocation of expenditure across exporters. Rows sum to one; there is no industry axis in this baseline.

02 · Match code to an equation

Select the block that computes the denominator of the trade-share equation in log space.

axis=1 sums over exporters within each importer row. Exponentiating log_denominator gives the trade-share denominator.

03 · Close the economy

Which equation determines relative wages, and why does the optimizer use only N−1 equations?

Exporter sales equal labor income: sum_n pi[n,i] w[n]L[n] = w[i]L[i]. World spending equals world income, making one market equation redundant. Fixing w[0]=1 removes nominal scale indeterminacy; all N residuals are still checked.

04 · Change the numeraire

If all wages are rescaled by a common positive factor, what happens?

The price index is homogeneous of degree one in wages. Common nominal scaling cancels from expenditure shares and w/P. Both verification routes must use the same wage normalization.

05 · Supply exact hats

Which information enters the exact-hat equilibrium equations for this trade-cost shock?

Baseline shares summarize technology and delivered-cost competitiveness. Baseline incomes supply spending weights. The equations do not need the counterfactual levels solution. The API also retains primitives to validate shocked iceberg costs.

06 · Explain two routes

Why should E1/E0 equal the exact-hat result, and what would agreement alone fail to prove?

Divide the counterfactual equations by their baseline counterparts with technology, labor, theta, and gamma fixed. Baseline shares then replace levels competitiveness terms. Identical closure, shock, and numeraire give the same equilibrium changes. Agreement alone does not prove convergence or rule out a shared implementation error.

07 · Diagnose a false pass

All comparison errors are 1e−13, but the hat solver reports success=false and a full residual of 2e−4. What is the verdict?

Convergence and equivalence are separate requirements. The solver must report success and the full residual norm must be below 1e−11; both error measures must also be below 1e−9.

08 · Interpret the shock

Trace the 10% bilateral trade-cost cut through shares, wages, prices, and real income. Why can country 2 also change?

Lower bilateral delivered costs initially increase the affected exporters' competitiveness. Expenditure shifts change sales and relative wages; those wage changes feed back into every importer's prices and shares. Welfare is the wage change divided by the price change. Country 2 participates in the same market-clearing system, so its outcomes can change even though its trade costs do not. Direction and magnitude should be read from the equilibrium, not assumed for every country.
